## Overview

Army division operations are integrated with several **joint processes**, especially targeting, airspace, collection/ISR, and air and missile defense. The Army operations process is not fully realized until Army planning and execution are synchronized with the corresponding joint processes.

Key joint processes in this lesson include:

- **Joint Air Tasking Cycle**
- **Joint Targeting Cycle**
- **Airspace control**
- **Joint Air-Ground Integration Center (JAGIC)** operations
- **Tactical Air Control System / Army Air-Ground System (TACS/AAGS)** integration

> **Remember:** Joint processes are synchronized, but they are **not necessarily sequential**.

## Key Authorities and Organizations

### Airspace Control Authority (ACA)

The **Airspace Control Authority (ACA)** develops the **Airspace Control Plan (ACP)** during the joint planning process in coordination with components and other joint forces.

- The **ACA develops the ACP**.
- The **Joint Force Commander (JFC) approves the ACP**.
- The ACP outlines authorities for airspace control.
- The JFC-designated ACA approves the **Coordinating Altitude (CA)**.

### Area Air Defense Commander (AADC)

The **Area Air Defense Commander (AADC)** is responsible for the joint force's air and missile defense planning and authorities.

Airspace-control authority delegated to a division does **not** include authorities vested in the AADC.

### Battlefield Coordination Detachment (BCD)

The **Battlefield Coordination Detachment (BCD)** is the Army component liaison located with the **Joint Force Air Component Commander (JFACC)** in the **Air Operations Center (AOC/JAOC)**.

The BCD:

- Represents Army/land-component requirements to the air component
- Processes land-force requirements for air support
- Monitors and interprets the land battle situation in the AOC
- Exchanges current operations and intelligence information between the land and air components
- Helps communicate Army target nominations and air support requirements into the Joint Air Tasking Cycle

> **Quick match:** **JFACC / AOC level = BCD**.

## TACS/AAGS

The **Tactical Air Control System / Army Air-Ground System (TACS/AAGS)** links Army and Air Force organizations from the joint level down to tactical units.

### Important Elements

| Echelon / Location      | Key Element                                         |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **JFACC / AOC**         | **BCD**                                             |
| **Division**            | **JAGIC, ASOC, TACP, Airspace Element, Fires Cell** |
| **Brigade Combat Team** | **TACP, Fires Cell, ADSE/AGIE**                     |
| **Battalion**           | **TACP, Fires Cell**                                |
| **Company**             | **JTAC / JFO**                                      |

### ASOC Linkage

The **Air Support Operations Center (ASOC)** is directly subordinate to the AOC and is an essential Air Force element within the division JAGIC.

The ASOC:

- Establishes and maintains the **Joint Air Request Net (JARN)**
- Processes immediate air support requests
- Communicates directly with the AOC and other decentralized execution elements such as AWACS and CRC
- Helps control air component aircraft operating in or near division-assigned airspace

## Joint Air-Ground Integration Center (JAGIC)

### Purpose

The **JAGIC** is a modular and scalable center designed to fully integrate and coordinate **fires and air operations** over and within the division commander's area of operations.

Its primary purpose is to **co-locate Army and Air Force decision-making authorities so they can integrate and control fires, airspace, and air operations during current operations**.

The JAGIC supports the maneuver commander's concept of operations while also meeting JFACC objectives and JFC-designated authorities.

> **Simple version:** Put the people who can make Army fires and Air Force airpower decisions together, with the same operational picture, so they can act quickly.

### JAGIC Location

The JAGIC is normally located in the division **Current Operations Integration Cell (COIC)**.

### Division-Assigned Airspace

**Division-assigned airspace** is:

> A volume of airspace in which the **Airspace Control Authority (ACA)** has delegated responsibility for control of that volume, in accordance with the **ACP** and **ACO**, to the **division commander**.

It is typically:

- Within the lateral boundaries of the division area of operations
- Up to the **Coordinating Altitude (CA)**

When delegated division-assigned airspace, the JAGIC controls and coordinates the use of that volume for Army and joint airspace users below the CA.

### Requesting Division-Assigned Airspace

Army divisions may request division-assigned airspace within their unit boundaries and below the CA when they can justify the requirement through:

- **Tactical necessity**
- **Demonstrated capability to control the airspace**
- A trained JAGIC/ASOC team capable of meeting ACP, ACO, and other airspace-control requirements

### Coordinating Altitude (CA)

The **Coordinating Altitude** is an **airspace control measure used to differentiate between control of airspace users above and below the coordinating altitude**.

Key points:

- The **JFC-designated ACA approves the CA**.
- The CA separates responsibility for controlling airspace users above and below it.
- The CA can change by **phase of the operation**.
- The CA does **not have to be the same across the entire joint operations area (JOA)**.
- Divisions should continually assess and justify their airspace and CA requirements.

> **Memory aid:** **CA = the dividing line for who controls airspace above versus below.**

## JAGIC and Air and Missile Defense

The AMD officer in the JAGIC:

- Works closely with the airspace element to resolve immediate and planned airspace conflicts
- Monitors AMD engagements in the division area of operations
- Understands air defense warnings, weapon control statuses, and identification authorities from the AADC
- Communicates relevant AMD guidance to the JAGIC and subordinate units
- Must understand division SHORAD employment and theater ADA assets operating inside the division area

The JAGIC must also understand the **Area Air Defense Plan (AADP)** because targets affecting air superiority and counterair operations may be inside or above division-assigned airspace.

## Joint Air Tasking Cycle

The **Joint Air Tasking Cycle** is the process used to plan, coordinate, task, execute, and assess joint air operations in support of the JFC's objectives.

The cycle normally includes approximately **72 hours of planning** followed by a **24-hour ATO execution period**.

### Six Stages

| Stage | Name                                       | Key Point / Product                                                                           |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Objectives, Effects, and Guidance**      | JFC guidance and air apportionment; **AOD** provides guidance for the ATO period              |
| **2** | **Target Development**                     | Component nominations are integrated and prioritized into the draft **JIPTL**                 |
| **3** | **Weaponeering and Allocation**            | **Master Air Attack Plan (MAAP)** is developed; resources are matched to targets and missions |
| **4** | **ATO Production and Dissemination**       | Produces and distributes the **ATO, ACO, and SPINS**                                          |
| **5** | **Execution Planning and Force Execution** | **ATO execution**; managed by the **Combat Operations Division (COD)**                        |
| **6** | **Assessment**                             | Evaluates results and feeds information into the next cycle                                   |

### Stage 1 — Objectives, Effects, and Guidance

The JFC establishes priorities and guidance for joint air operations. The JFACC provides an air apportionment recommendation to the JFC, and the approved guidance is reflected in the **Air Operations Directive (AOD)**.

### Stage 2 — Target Development

Components submit target nominations and air support requirements. The nominations are validated, integrated, and prioritized to create a draft **Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List (JIPTL)**.

The JFC is the approval authority for the JIPTL.

### Stage 3 — Weaponeering and Allocation

During **Stage 3**, the **Master Air Attack Plan (MAAP)** is developed.

The MAAP team:

- Matches available air capabilities and resources to targets and missions
- Allocates sorties and weapon systems
- Builds strike packages and mission flows
- Integrates support requirements such as fuel, C2, SEAD, ISR, and personnel recovery

### Stage 4 — ATO Production and Dissemination

The ATO Production Team converts the MAAP into executable products and publishes/disseminates:

- **Air Tasking Order (ATO)**
- **Airspace Control Order (ACO)**
- **Special Instructions (SPINS)**

### Stage 5 — Execution Planning and Force Execution

**Stage 5** focuses on execution of the ATO and is managed by the **Combat Operations Division (COD)**.

During Stage 5, the COD:

- Monitors ATO execution
- Directs dynamic events
- Conducts or coordinates dynamic targeting
- Retargets or reroles aircraft as required
- Publishes changes to the ATO and ACO
- Uses decentralized C2 elements such as ASOC, CRC, AWACS, and WOC
- Responds to immediate air support requests

### Stage 6 — Assessment

Assessment occurs throughout the cycle but is formally identified as Stage 6.

Assessment includes:

- Battle damage assessment (BDA)
- Munitions effectiveness assessment (MEA)
- Reattack recommendations
- Operational assessment that informs future apportionment and allocation decisions

The assessment stage feeds the next Joint Air Tasking Cycle.

### Stage 3 vs. Stage 5

| Stage       | What Happens                                                       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Stage 3** | **MAAP is developed** — planning, weaponeering, and allocation     |
| **Stage 5** | **ATO is executed** — Combat Operations Division manages execution |
